I exported my bookmarks: 417 links, 356 domains, collected over several years.
Half of it turned out to be rubbish - one-off articles, hosting panels, forgotten
tabs. What follows is what survived the clean-up.

Every entry has a link, a line on what it is for, and a fact that makes it
interesting in its own right. I checked the numbers today rather than copying
them from someone else's list. Every link is alive.

## Colour

[realtimecolors.com](https://realtimecolors.com) - pick a palette and see it on a
finished landing page mock-up rather than on swatches.

> It solves the core problem with colour generators: four pretty squares tell you
> almost nothing about how text will read against a background. Here you look
> straight at buttons, headings and small type.

[uicolors.app](https://uicolors.app/create) - turns one hex into a full 50 to 950
scale in Tailwind format.

> Exactly the format `@theme` expects in Tailwind v4: copy and paste. Building
> that scale by hand takes half an hour and still comes out uneven.

[hypercolor.dev](https://hypercolor.dev) - ready-made gradients as Tailwind
classes.

[grainient.supply](https://grainient.supply) - over a thousand gradients with
grain.

> The grain is not decoration but a technique: a clean gradient across a large
> block bands because of limited bits per channel, and noise hides it. Apple uses
> the same trick in macOS backgrounds.

[fffuel.co](https://fffuel.co) - SVG generators: waves, grids, blobs, noise.

> A dozen and a half separate tools on one domain, all exporting clean SVG with
> no dependencies.

## Icons and graphics

[icones.js.org](https://icones.js.org) - search across every Iconify set at once.

> I checked through their API today: **236 sets and 334,616 icons** in a single
> search. It hands you SVG, JSX or a name for `astro-icon` in one click.

[simpleicons.org](https://simpleicons.org) - brand logos in a single colour.

> 3,400 icons under CC0, so you can use them anywhere without attribution. Each
> carries its official brand colour, which saves you eyedropping someone's site.

[svgl.app](https://svgl.app) - company logos in SVG, with dark variants.

> The dark variants are what make it more useful than the alternatives: on a dark
> background an ordinary logo often collapses into a black blob.

[tabler.io](https://tabler.io/icons) - six thousand icons from one set.

> 6,150 of them, all drawn with a 2px stroke on a 24 by 24 grid. When the whole
> interface comes from one set it looks assembled rather than glued together.

[thiings.co](https://thiings.co) - a collection of 3D objects in one style.

[undraw.co](https://undraw.co) - illustrations with a swappable accent colour.

> Change the hex on the site and every illustration recolours to your brand
> before you download it. The licence allows commercial use without attribution.

## Where to look when nothing comes

[godly.website](https://godly.website) - a strictly curated collection of sites.

> It differs from Awwwards in having no nominations and no voting. One person
> with taste collects what they consider good.

[minimal.gallery](https://minimal.gallery) - the same, leaning minimal.

[refero.design](https://refero.design) - references by element rather than by
whole site.

> You search for "how an empty state looks" or "where search lives", not "a
> pretty landing page". It saves time when the question is narrow.

[mobbin.com](https://mobbin.com) - the same for mobile apps.

> Screenshots are arranged by flow: not one screen but the whole chain from
> sign-up to payment. You can see how many steps competitors take.

[darkmodedesign.com](https://darkmodedesign.com) - dark interfaces only.

> Useful because dark themes break differently from light ones: shadows stop
> working and borders have to be built out of lightness instead.

[cofolios.com](https://cofolios.com) - portfolios of designers at large
companies.

## Component libraries

[heroui.com](https://heroui.com) - the former NextUI, third version.

> The only mainstream one that takes surface layers and border tokens seriously:
> they are derived by mixing the background with the foreground rather than
> written by hand. A border therefore cannot go invisible on a new theme.

[ui.shadcn.com](https://ui.shadcn.com) - the de facto standard for React.

> Formally it is not a library at all: components are not installed as a package
> but copied as source into your project. That is why you can edit them and why
> updates break nothing.

[21st.dev](https://21st.dev) - a catalogue of components by design engineers.

[magicui.design](https://magicui.design) - animated blocks.

> Take them one at a time. A site assembled entirely from these looks like a
> thousand other landing pages: the effects are too recognisable.

[uiverse.io](https://uiverse.io) - buttons and loaders from the community.

[preline.co](https://preline.co) - a large set of Tailwind blocks.

## Small things that save time

[carbon.now.sh](https://carbon.now.sh) - an image from a chunk of code.

[opengraph.xyz](https://www.opengraph.xyz) - shows how a link will unfurl in
Telegram, X and Slack.

> Before publishing rather than after. It is separately useful for showing
> several networks side by side: they want different image proportions.

[favicon.io](https://favicon.io) - favicons from text, an image or an emoji.

[shields.io](https://shields.io) - those README badges.

> The badges are dynamic: package version, bundle size and download counts pull
> themselves and never go stale the way a hand-typed number does.

[readme.so](https://readme.so) - assemble a README from blocks.

[cobalt.tools](https://cobalt.tools) - download video or audio from anywhere.

> Open source, 42 thousand stars on GitHub, AGPL. It stores nothing: the file is
> assembled on the fly and handed straight to you.

[shots.so](https://shots.so) - mock-ups: a screenshot inside a device, on a
background.

[easing.dev](https://easing.dev) - animation curves plotted side by side.

> Seeing them next to each other explains the difference between `ease-out` and
> `ease-in-out` in a second, where documentation takes a paragraph.

[devdocs.io](https://devdocs.io) - documentation for dozens of languages in one
place.

> It works offline: the doc sets download into the browser. Priceless on a plane
> or a train, where you need documentation exactly when there is no network.

## Technically beautiful

These are more interesting to open than to describe.

[shaders.paper.design](https://shaders.paper.design) - shader backgrounds with
zero dependencies.

> It loads in a second and looks more expensive than half the sites that drag in
> a heavy WebGL library for the same effect.

[unicorn.studio](https://unicorn.studio) - a no-code editor for WebGL effects.

[rive.app](https://rive.app) - interactive animation with state machines.

> Not video and not Lottie: the animation has a state machine and reacts to
> input. The runtime is written in C++ and runs on the web, on mobile and in
> games.

[haptics.lochie.me](https://haptics.lochie.me) - haptic feedback on the mobile
web.

> Open it on a phone or there is no point at all. It demonstrates something most
> people do not know exists on the web.

[torph.lochie.me](https://torph.lochie.me) - dependency-free text morphing.

## Products with exemplary interfaces

Nothing to download here, but worth staring at.

[linear.app](https://linear.app) - still the benchmark for speed and keyboard
navigation.

> Half of today's issue trackers are an attempt to reproduce it. They also
> publish the "Linear Method": how they run development, not just how the
> interface looks.

[family.co](https://family.co) - a crypto wallet with the best animation in
fintech.

[raycast.com](https://raycast.com) - a desktop app made with respect for detail.

[ghostty.org](https://ghostty.org) - a terminal whose documentation site is
itself exemplary.

> Written in Zig, 60 thousand stars on GitHub, and the key difference from its
> rivals: a native interface on every platform instead of Electron. That is where
> the speed comes from.

[cal.com](https://cal.com) - the open alternative to Calendly.

## If you build with Astro

[docs.astro.build](https://docs.astro.build) - documentation you keep coming back
to.

> Reading it beats googling recipes: half of the "strange" problems are described
> there in plain words. Confirmed twice this week on my own work.

[ui.full.dev](https://ui.full.dev) - components built for Astro rather than React
wrapped in Astro.

[inox-tools.fryuni.dev](https://inox-tools.fryuni.dev) - integrations that patch
gaps in the platform.

[npmx.dev](https://npmx.dev) - an npm package browser.

> It shows weight, the dependency tree and file contents at once, without hopping
> between npm, GitHub and bundlephobia.

[risingstars.js.org](https://risingstars.js.org) - what took off during the year.

> It counts growth over the year rather than absolute stars, so new projects come
> out on top instead of perennial leaders like React.

## Comments and content for static sites

Relevant if you run a static site and want something alive on it.

[remark42.com](https://remark42.com) - comments on your own server.

> Written in Go, deploys as one container, tracks nobody. The author is Umputun,
> host of the Radio-T podcast, and the code is noticeably tidier than average.

[cusdis.com](https://cusdis.com) - a lighter open alternative.

[pagescms.org](https://pagescms.org) - an admin panel for content that lives in
git.

> One config file goes into the repository, the panel itself is hosted by them.
> Nothing to deploy.

[keystatic.com](https://keystatic.com) - the same, with a TypeScript schema.

> An important detail for static sites: the admin panel needs a server runtime,
> so a purely static site has to move to hybrid rendering for it.

## Simply pleasant

[asoftmurmur.com](https://asoftmurmur.com) - background sounds of rain and wind.

> A mixer with several tracks: blend rain with distant thunder and mute the
> birds. It beats "focus" playlists because there is no melody for attention to
> latch onto.

[thanks.dev](https://thanks.dev) - donations to library authors.

> It walks your repository's dependency graph and splits an amount across the
> people whose code you actually use, transitive packages you never heard of
> included.

[notbyai.fyi](https://notbyai.fyi) - a "made by a human" badge.

[symbl.cc](https://symbl.cc) - emoji and Unicode characters with decent search.

---

The general observation from the clean-up: out of 417 bookmarks I regularly open
about twenty. The rest is either "might come in handy one day" that never came,
or a task solved some other way. Doing this once a year is worth it if only to
see what you actually use.
