A reader in a channel sees a link to an article. What follows is the usual
routine: a tap, Telegram opens its in-app browser, the browser pulls HTML, fonts,
images and scripts, shows a cookie banner and jolts the header once the font
lands. Three or four seconds on a decent connection, and noticeably more on the
underground.

Instant View removes that step entirely. One tap and the article is already
open: text, headings, images, code. In the same typeface as the rest of Telegram
and in the same theme, light or dark, that the reader has set.

## What the reader gets

**Speed.** The page is served by Telegram from its own cache, not by your host. A
reader will not feel the difference between an article on a fast CDN and one on
slow shared hosting. Both open the same way.

**The client's typography.** Instant View is not your layout but a structure:
heading, paragraph, quote, code, image with a caption. Telegram renders it with
its own typeface and spacing. Your fonts, colours and grid do not travel.

**The theme.** If the reader runs dark mode, the article opens dark even when the
site is light-only. My blog, for instance, has no dark theme at all, and in
Instant View it gets one for free.

**Nothing extra.** No cookie banner, no subscription pop-up, no header that
sticks to the scroll. The format simply has nowhere to put them.

## What the reader does not get

The flip side of the same property: **no JavaScript runs in Instant View**.
Nothing interactive from your site makes it across.

On this blog, for example, code blocks carry a header with a "Copy code" button
and an "Open" menu that sends the snippet to an AI service with a ready-made
prompt. In Instant View none of that exists and none of it can - what remains is
plain monospace text. The giscus comments do not travel either: they are an
iframe from GitHub.

Which leads to the thing that matters most for an author: **Instant View takes
the reader away from your site**. No visit, no analytics, no comment, no click
through to a project. The reader read it and stayed in the messenger.

For a channel, where the goal is that people read the thing, that is the right
trade. For a site that lives on traffic, think it through.

## How it works

The key thing to understand up front: **a template is written for a domain, not
for a page**.

You describe once where the heading sits on your pages, where the body of the
article is, and what should be thrown away. From then on the template applies to
every address matching its pattern. New posts land in Instant View by themselves,
as long as the markup has not changed.

The template language is XPath plus functions. Here is this blog's working
template in full:

```txt
~version: "2.1"

?path: /articles/.+
?path: /en/articles/.+

body: //article

title:          $body//h1
subtitle:       //meta[@property="og:description"]/@content
author:         //meta[@name="author"]/@content
published_date: //meta[@property="article:published_time"]/@content

cover: $body//img[contains(@class, "aspect-video")]

@remove: $body//a[contains(@class, "select-none")]
@remove: $body//div[contains(@class, "code-block-header")]
@remove: $body//img[contains(@class, "project-favicon")]
```

Twenty lines. Half of them say "take this from the meta tags", the other half say
"throw the interface away".

## Two tiers, and the second is not for everyone

This is where many people stop without working it out, and that is a shame.

**Tier one: your own link.** As soon as the template is written and works, the
editor hands you an address shaped like
`t.me/iv?url=<article address>&rhash=<template hash>`. That link opens in Instant
View for everyone, immediately. No moderation needed. The `rhash` is one per
domain, so for a new post only the address part changes.

For a channel that is enough: post that link and you are done.

**Tier two: moderation.** You need it only if you want Instant View to appear on
**any** link to your domain that someone drops into a chat, without the `t.me/iv`
wrapper. Here the template goes into Telegram's review queue: you mark several
verified pages, the wait is unpredictable and rejection is possible.

The practical difference: tier one covers your channel, tier two covers all of
Telegram.

## What the site needs

Nothing special. Instant View reads ordinary HTML, and the more semantic it is,
the shorter your template:

- the article wrapped in `<article>`, so `body` is one line;
- `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:image` present;
- `article:published_time` present, otherwise you parse the date out of the text;
- images in `<figure>` with `<figcaption>`, so captions arrive as captions;
- code in `<pre><code>`, so it arrives as code.

There is no need for a separate markup layer aimed at Instant View. If your HTML
is decent, the template comes out short.

## Three traps

Now the part this text exists for. I collected all three personally.

### There is no `@unwrap` function

It seems obvious: there is a wrapper, remove it and keep the children. I wrote
`@unwrap` and the editor answered:

```txt
Unknown function: @unwrap
FUNCTION_NOT_FOUND: unwrap
```

Instant View 2.1 has **no such function at all**. There is `@remove`, which
deletes a node together with its contents, plus `@replace_tag`, `@wrap`,
`@wrap_inner` and `@detach`. Nothing unwraps.

The good news is that you usually do not need it. Instant View looks for `<pre>`,
`<table>` and `<figure>` across the whole body and parses them regardless of how
many `div` layers they sit inside. I deleted the three `@unwrap` lines and
everything fell into place.

### `//img[1]` is not the first image on the page

The classic XPath trap that catches everyone who writes it once a year.

```txt
cover: $body//img[1]
```

It reads as "the first image in the article" and means "every image that comes
first inside its own parent". In my post about tatarverse there are **23** of
those.

To get the first image of the document you need brackets - `($body//img)[1]` - or,
more reliably, a selector by attribute:

```txt
cover: $body//img[contains(@class, "aspect-video")]
```

Only the cover carries `aspect-video`. I checked across every post: exactly one
match in each.

### Small icons turn into rubbish

The least obvious one. In my articles, external links and project cards pull in
the domain's favicon, and every code block has a menu with service icons. On the
site those are 16 by 16 pixel images sitting inline.

Instant View does not know they are decorative. To it they are `<img>` elements,
and each becomes **a separate image block** in the article. I counted in the built
HTML: between 12 and 36 such icons in a single post.

Three `@remove` lines by class fix it. But you can only notice it in the editor's
preview: on the site those icons look perfectly fine.

## You can only test it in the editor

One last thing worth knowing in advance: **a template cannot be run locally**.
There is no linter, no tests, no way to render Instant View on your own machine.
The only check is the preview inside the editor at instantview.telegram.org, and
you sign in there with a Telegram account.

The practical takeaway: do not write the whole template in your head the way I
did. Open the editor, paste five lines, look, add five more. I wrote twenty lines
blind and collected three errors at once.

## Is it worth it

If you run a channel and drop links to your own articles into it - yes,
unambiguously. Half an hour on a template, after which it works by itself for
every future post, and the reader gets the article instantly, in their own theme.

If your site lives on ads or conversions, do the maths. Instant View honestly
takes the visit for itself.
